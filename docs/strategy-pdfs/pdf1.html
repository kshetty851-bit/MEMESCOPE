MEMESCOPE System Architecture & Default Trading Strategy

MEMESCOPE is an opportunity intelligence platform for the Solana meme-coin market. A scanner answers “what exists”. MEMESCOPE answers “what became interesting, and why now” — then commits that answer to a permanent, immutable record before the outcome is known.

The problem

Thousands of tokens launch daily. Almost all are worthless. Claims about which ones mattered are made after the fact, when they cannot be checked.

The method

Every judgement is written down before the outcome, with a stated rule, a timestamp, and the evidence it was based on. Missing data is charged, never estimated.

The proof

Paper wallets trade those judgements mechanically with real execution quotes. An empty board is a truthful board. Failed calls stay visible.

02The four planes

Services are grouped by what they are allowed to do. The boundary matters: the data plane never decides a trade, the trading plane never writes market history, and the operations plane may read everything and, today, change nothing.

Data plane

Ingests and stores what the market did. Writes tokens, snapshots, scores, Radar.

Trading plane

Reads market history, writes only its own wallet tables. Two isolated wallets.

Operations plane

Probes health, opens incidents, writes audit rows. Executes no repair.

UI plane

Next.js frontend and FastAPI, behind Caddy and the alpha access gate.

Correction to the assumed flow. There is no standalone “Nursery” pipeline stage between Discovery and Radar. The nursery is a priority lane inside enrichment that polls newly discovered tokens more often. Likewise, security (SEC-2) is not a pipeline stage before Track Record — it is an entry precondition applied inside the wallet, at the moment a position would be opened.

05What $100 actually does

Three price paths, one rule set. The running peak is stored on the position row and only ever moves up; the trailing threshold is always 75% of it. The position closes at the first breach, and the money it returns is what a route would actually pay — not what the trigger said.

Why a 25% trailing stop does not cap the loss at 25%

1 · A stop is a trigger, not a fill. The rule says when to sell. The market says at what. Production deliberately books the executable quote, never the trigger level — so if the price gapped straight through the threshold, the fill is below it by however far it gapped.

2 · The market is sampled, not watched. Positions are evaluated once a minute against snapshots that are themselves polled. Between two readings a meme-coin pool can be drained entirely. Nothing traded at the threshold, so nothing could have sold there.

3 · The threshold is 25% off the peak, not off cost. A position that ran to 1.80× and gave back 25% is a +35% winner. A position that never rose gives back 25% of the entry — and if the pool dies first, the provider marks it terminal and it returns $0.00.

Evidence, not assertion: the sibling Karthik wallet — same execution path, same market, exit target instead of trailing stop — has closed 30 positions at exactly $0.00, on the provider's own report that the pool had nothing left to sell into. That outcome is available to this wallet on identical terms.

Also: the 6-hour cut

Since 2026-08-21 every new position carries an absolute expires_at. A position still climbing at hour six is sold anyway, at whatever is executable. 11 of 240 closed trades exited this way.

Costs are charged, not assumed away

Fills come from live Jupiter routes (50 bps slippage tolerance). Where a route is unavailable the fallback model charges 30 bps per side plus exact constant-product impact S²/(L/2).

06How this loses money, and how it breaks

Nothing here is hypothetical framing. Each item names the mechanism, what production does about it today, and what remains exposed.

Rug / gap-through

Liquidity is pulled between two snapshots; the trailing threshold is never traded.

Mitigated: the fill is the executable quote, so the loss is booked honestly. Exposed: the loss itself is uncapped — up to the full $100.

Liquidity collapse

The pool thins until no route will quote the position at all.

Mitigated: a pool reported inactive closes at $0.00, not at its last printed price. Exposed: that is a real, total loss — not a bookkeeping artefact.

Stale market data

A position judged against a reading older than the market it claims to describe.

Mitigated: exits replay every unseen snapshot in timestamp order, and expiry is measured against each observation's own timestamp — never a wall clock. A worker that missed six hours produces the same trades as one that missed none.

Feed outage

Enrichment or a provider stops; positions stop being re-priced.

Mitigated: positions are held and said to be held, never closed on absent data; HQ opens an incident. Exposed: during the gap no exit rule can fire, whatever the market does.

Insufficient cash · capital saturation

Qualified tokens arrive with no money left to buy them.

The dominant condition today: 897 of 1,159 refusals in 24 h were insufficient_paper_cash. With no take-profit and no fixed stop, capital sits in open positions until a trailing stop or the 6-hour clock releases it.

Execution price vs trigger price

The price that fires a rule is not the price that fills it.

Production stores both — exit_observed_price and exit_price. On the sibling wallet, average entry price impact was 10.18%, exit impact 0.87%. An order against a thin pool moves the price it is trying to get.

SEC-2 limitation

The gate proves specific, checkable properties. It is not a safety verdict.

It cannot detect a coordinated exit by holders, a social rug, or a team that simply stops. LP holders are not resolved — an outstanding LP supply is UNKNOWN, which refuses the buy without calling the token unsafe.

Young-token risk

The Radar has no minimum age and, by design, no market-cap floor.

Admission claims something changed, not that a project is durable. Bonding-curve pairs report no liquidity, so they are refused rather than costed against an assumed depth — correct, and it removes a whole class of otherwise-eligible tokens.

Two failure modes specific to this deployment, found during verification

FindingConsequence
The wallet row records trailing_stop_25_v1, but the wallet trades trailing_stop_25_secured_hold6h_v3. The repository's SEC-2 backstop keys off the row, and trailing_stop_25_v1 is not in the gated set — so the last-line defence is inert for the live wallet. The service-level gate is working (27 security_gate refusals in 24 h), but the defence-in-depth check built to catch a future code path that skipped it is not armed.
PAPER_WALLET_STRATEGY_ID is set to a retired strategy id. The service logs paper_strategy_unknown on every pass and falls back to the registry default. The wallet trades correctly by accident of a safe fallback, not because it was configured to.

Assumptions & limitations

  • Wallet figures are a snapshot at 2026-08-22 14:55 UTC. Both wallets trade every minute; the numbers have moved since.
  • Page 5's price paths illustrate the verified rules; they are not replays of specific production trades.
  • Refusal counts are the trailing 24 h of paper_decision_snapshots. Execution-impact figures are averaged over Karthik's 132 entries, which share this wallet's execution client but not its $100 order size.
  • The API could not be queried programmatically — the alpha gate rejects scripted sign-in. Figures come from production source, container environment and database, which are the facts the API renders.
  • Production was redeployed twice while this was being written — bcc5550 → 11fe507 (disk only) → 4b7f89e (built and served at 14:32 UTC, which is when Agent Karthik went live). Every statement here describes the image serving at 14:55 UTC.
  • No production data, configuration, strategy or wallet was altered to produce this document.
